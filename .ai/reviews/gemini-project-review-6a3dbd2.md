## Raw output

### 1. Verdict

At commit `6a3dbd2`, this is an unusually disciplined prototype-to-architecture codebase whose core distributed invariants—authority-owned state vs. untrusted member repos, idempotent step-journaled operator mutations (`src/operator/journal.ts:54-134`), confidential `private_key_jwt` authority grant custody (`src/operator/authority-grant.ts:1-367`), lexicon-backed record validation (`src/protocol/registry.ts:37-280`), and clean-room protocol-sufficiency proofs (`src/reader/independent.ts:1-316`, `src/reader/independent-dm.ts:1-168`)—are genuinely sound, but it is still a chat client rather than a third-party app platform and carries sharp operational and security edges as it scales. What is fragile today is unbounded outbound PDS/PLC I/O inside heartbeated SQLite locks, `authorityDid`-wide locking and channel-key coupling, overly broad permanent-failure classification on federated repos, missing CI and rate-limited PDS testing, and several concrete defects in DM leave state, self-revoking operator replays, and lexicon parity. What will hurt most as it grows—especially once R4–R9 push sync and wildcard `authority=*` tokens into the browser and introduce third-party apps—is the combination of 200-writer browser fan-out against real PDS rate limits, `'unsafe-inline'` CSP alongside plaintext IndexedDB history, destructive bot lifecycle semantics (where uninstalling or rotating a bot erases its historical messages), and repeated authority re-consent cliffs every time a platform collection is added.
